# Multi-Regional SEO: How to Grow Across European Markets

Language: English

Canonical HTML page: https://raccoon-studio.com.ua/en/blog/multi-regional-seo/

> Learn how to expand SEO across European cities and countries using regional landing pages, Google Business Profile, hreflang and a practical site structure.

SEO

When a company starts serving customers beyond its original city, its website needs to support that expansion.

Oleksandr Padalko · 17/11/2026 · 7 min read

![SEO Raccoon analyzing multi-regional website optimization across Europe](https://raccoon-studio.com.ua/images/blog/multi-regional-seo-raccoon-studio.webp)

Imagine a service business based in Wrocław that also operates in Warsaw, Poznań and Kraków.

Or a Ukrainian company entering Poland, with plans to reach customers in Czechia and Germany.

Should the business create a separate website for every location? Would subdomains work better? Or should everything remain on one domain?

I would not begin with the technical structure.

I would begin by understanding how customers search in each market.

## Local search depends on what people are looking for

When someone searches for "air conditioning repair", Google may use their location to identify relevant nearby businesses.

The results in Warsaw may look different from those in Wrocław.

A search such as "how does a heat pump work" usually has a different intent.

The user is looking for information rather than a local service provider.

However, search queries do not fall into perfectly separated categories.

Google considers the query, location, language, relevance and other signals.

The important question is what the user expects to find.

A nearby company? An online store? A practical guide? A comparison of services?

That distinction should influence the structure and content of the website.

### Searches that include a city name

Consider the difference between "Polish language courses" and "Polish language courses Wrocław".

The second query explicitly identifies a location.

But repeating the word Wrocław throughout a page is not a substitute for useful information.

A strong local landing page should explain what is available, who the service is intended for and how customers can use it.

Location details should support the content rather than dominate it.

## Does every city need its own landing page?

Imagine a technical service company operating throughout Poland.

Its homepage says that it serves Warsaw, Wrocław, Poznań and Kraków.

That is a reasonable starting point.

However, a customer in Warsaw may want specific information about technician availability, service coverage and how an appointment works in that city.

If the company can provide meaningful local details, a dedicated page may be useful.

But it needs to offer more than a different city name.

Creating hundreds of almost identical pages is not an effective long-term strategy.

When pages are produced primarily to capture similar searches and funnel visitors toward the same destination, they may also raise concerns under Google's doorway abuse policy.

## Choosing the right website structure

There are several common approaches.

### Regional pages on one domain

For example:

example.pl/services/wroclaw/

example.pl/services/warsaw/

example.pl/services/poznan/

For many service businesses, this is a practical solution.

Content, analytics, navigation and technical maintenance remain within one website.

It is also easier to maintain consistent branding and user experience.

However, every regional page should have a clear purpose.

If the company does not have a physical office in Warsaw, it should not claim to have one.

A service-area business can explain how it serves customers without inventing local premises.

### Regional subdomains

For example:

warsaw.example.com

wroclaw.example.com

Subdomains may be appropriate when regional operations use different catalogues, systems or content management teams.

But they can add unnecessary complexity for smaller businesses.

Using a subdomain does not automatically improve Google rankings.

### Separate country or regional domains

Separate websites may make sense when a company operates distinct brands or business entities across countries.

For example, a business may have significantly different operations in Poland and Germany.

In that situation, separate domains can reflect real organizational differences.

But creating a new domain for every city solely for SEO is rarely the first option I would recommend.

Every website requires maintenance, useful content, analytics and ongoing development.

Networks of nearly identical sites can create additional quality and duplication problems.

## Regional SEO and international SEO are different challenges

Expanding from Wrocław to Warsaw is not the same as expanding from Poland to Germany.

International SEO requires additional planning.

Language, currency, legal requirements, customer expectations and local competition may all differ.

Even the terminology used to describe the same service can vary between markets.

This is why translating keywords directly from one language to another is not enough.

Keyword research should reflect the language and search habits of the target audience.

## Managing language versions correctly

Hreflang is an important part of multilingual SEO.

It helps search engines understand which language or regional version of a page is intended for a particular audience.

For example, a Polish page targeting Poland may use pl-PL, a Ukrainian version may use uk-UA, and a general English page may use en.

These values should reflect the actual target audience.

Hreflang does not replace translation and does not guarantee rankings.

Every version still needs useful content, correct language settings and appropriate canonical URLs.

When different language pages are intended to be indexed separately, they should generally use their own canonical URLs.

## Google Business Profile and genuine local presence

Google Business Profile can be valuable for companies serving local customers.

It allows businesses to provide contact information, services, opening hours, photographs and customer reviews.

But business information must be accurate.

Creating fictional offices in different cities is not a legitimate local SEO strategy.

Businesses that visit customers should follow Google's service-area business guidelines and use appropriate address visibility settings.

Adding a city to a service area does not guarantee strong rankings in that city.

Google's local results consider relevance, distance and prominence.

## Why copying large competitors can be misleading

Major marketplaces and international chains often have thousands of regional landing pages.

But their business model may be very different from that of a small service company.

A large retailer might operate local warehouses, branches, inventories and delivery systems.

In that context, extensive regional pages can serve a genuine purpose.

A smaller company should not copy the same architecture simply because the larger competitor ranks well.

I prefer to study businesses with similar services, resources and customer needs.

The goal is to understand how competitors communicate their offer, not to reproduce their website structure.

## Researching demand across different markets

I would begin with a list of locations where the company actually operates or plans to expand.

Then I would research relevant searches in each market.

In Poland, that might include Warsaw, Wrocław, Poznań and Kraków.

For expansion into Germany or Czechia, I would conduct separate research using local languages and terminology.

Next, I would review the search results and identify the types of pages that satisfy each query.

Are people looking for local providers, online retailers, comparison platforms or educational content?

Only after that would I decide which regional pages are worth building.

Not every city requires a dedicated landing page.

Not every country requires a separate domain.

## Measuring performance after launch

The number of regional pages published is not a useful success metric by itself.

I would focus on impressions, clicks, qualified organic traffic, enquiries and conversions.

Google Search Console provides valuable search performance data, although it does not offer perfect city-level reporting for every visitor.

Conversion tracking can be handled through a properly configured analytics platform, with appropriate privacy safeguards.

It is also worth checking whether multiple pages unnecessarily compete for the same queries without providing meaningful differences.

If a regional page performs poorly, the first step should be diagnosis.

Publishing ten more similar pages is not a substitute for understanding the problem.

## Final thoughts

Multi-regional SEO is not about creating the largest possible collection of landing pages.

It is about helping people in different locations find relevant and accurate information.

A website should clearly explain where a company operates, which services are available and how customers can access them.

For many businesses, one well-organized domain with useful regional and language-specific pages will be easier to manage than a network of separate websites.

However, the right choice depends on the business model, search demand and the ability to maintain meaningful content.

A company should not expand its SEO architecture faster than it can genuinely serve the markets it wants to reach.
