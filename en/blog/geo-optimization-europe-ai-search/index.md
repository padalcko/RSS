*A few years ago, business owners mainly wanted to know one thing: “Where does my website rank on Google?” Today, a different question is becoming increasingly relevant: “Why does ChatGPT mention my competitors but never recommend my company?”*

This is where Generative Engine Optimization, or GEO, comes into the picture.

Imagine someone opening a beauty clinic in Poland. They need a company that can service professional laser equipment.

Instead of browsing ten different websites, they ask an AI assistant:

“Which companies repair diode laser equipment in Poland?”

Or imagine an international student moving to Wrocław:

“Where can I find Polish language courses for foreigners in Wrocław?”

An AI-powered search system may respond with a short list of businesses, descriptions of their services and links to relevant sources.

The question is no longer just whether your website appears in search results.

It is also whether AI-powered systems can find, understand and reference reliable information about your business.

That is what GEO aims to improve.

## What Is GEO and How Is It Different from SEO?

GEO stands for Generative Engine Optimization.

It describes efforts to improve how information about a business, brand or website is discovered and represented in AI-generated search responses.

Traditional SEO focuses on visibility in search engines. GEO extends that work to AI-powered search experiences.

The two approaches are closely connected.

A website still needs accessible pages, useful content, clear navigation, relevant information and a solid technical foundation.

What changes is how users interact with search.

Instead of entering a short keyword phrase and opening several websites, people can ask detailed questions and receive a synthesized answer.

However, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and Google AI Mode are different products. They do not use identical search and retrieval mechanisms.

Appearing in one system does not guarantee visibility in another.

There is also no universal submission form that guarantees your business will be recommended by an AI assistant.

## Why GEO Matters for European Businesses

Europe is not one uniform search market.

A company operating in Poland faces different languages, customer expectations and competitive conditions from a business in Germany, France or the United Kingdom.

Even companies offering the same service may need very different content strategies.

Consider a technical service provider operating in Wrocław.

Its customers may search in Polish for “serwis laserów kosmetycznych” or “naprawa laserów diodowych”.

An international customer may instead ask:

“Where can I find professional laser equipment maintenance in Poland?”

The underlying service is similar, but the search intent, language and expected answer can differ.

For companies operating across borders, GEO therefore needs to be developed alongside multilingual and international SEO.

Simply translating an English website into several languages is rarely enough.

Businesses need to understand how customers actually describe their problems in each target market.

## 1. Build a Website That Search Systems Can Understand

Before investing in GEO, start with the technical foundations of your website.

Can search engines access your important pages? Are those pages indexed? Does each service have a clear description? Can visitors easily find your business information?

These questions may sound basic, but many companies still struggle with them.

A website might list five different services in a single paragraph without explaining what each service involves.

For a business repairing professional beauty equipment, it is more useful to provide dedicated pages covering diode laser repair, cooling system maintenance, IPL handpiece restoration and electronic diagnostics.

Each page should address a specific customer need.

Technical elements such as semantic HTML, logical headings, internal links, XML sitemaps and appropriate Schema.org structured data can support search engines in understanding the website.

Structured data is useful, but it does not guarantee that an AI assistant will cite or recommend your business.

## 2. Create Content Around Real Customer Questions

Traditional keyword research remains important.

However, AI-powered search encourages businesses to think beyond short keyword phrases.

A customer may not simply search for “laser repair Poland”.

They may ask:

“Why is my diode laser handpiece overheating?”

“How much does professional laser equipment maintenance cost in Poland?”

“Can a cosmetic laser be repaired without shipping it back to the manufacturer?”

These are specific questions that require useful answers.

The same principle applies to other industries.

A language school can explain how international students prepare for a Polish B1 language examination.

An automotive repair business can publish a guide explaining which types of dents can be repaired using paintless dent repair.

A software company can explain how its product solves a particular operational problem.

The goal is not to produce hundreds of nearly identical articles.

It is to publish information that helps people make informed decisions.

## 3. Demonstrate Real Expertise Through Original Content

AI systems can generate general explanations about almost any common subject.

What they cannot independently verify is the detailed history of a repair completed by your technicians last week.

This is why original content matters.

Consider two articles.

The first is titled “Professional Laser Repair Services” and contains a few generic paragraphs.

The second describes an actual equipment failure, the symptoms reported by the customer, the diagnostic process, the identified problem and the repair that was completed.

The second article provides more specific information.

It is useful to potential customers, technical specialists and anyone researching a similar issue.

This principle applies to virtually every industry.

Businesses can publish documented case studies, original photographs, practical tutorials, technical measurements and detailed explanations based on real experience.

## 4. Build a Credible Presence Beyond Your Own Website

Your website is not the only place where people can learn about your company.

Business profiles, independent reviews, industry publications, professional directories and local media can all provide additional information.

For a local business in Poland, maintaining an accurate Google Business Profile is an obvious starting point.

Depending on the industry, relevant platforms might include Booksy, ZnanyLekarz, Clutch, G2, Capterra or specialized trade directories.

Not every platform is relevant to every business.

A local beauty clinic has different needs from a European software company.

The goal should be to build a genuine presence on platforms that customers actually use.

Industry publications and expert contributions can also help establish a stronger public information footprint.

But there is an important distinction between improving your online presence and guaranteeing AI recommendations.

No individual review platform or publication can promise that ChatGPT will recommend your business.

## 5. Avoid Fake Reviews and Manipulated Rankings

Some GEO strategies encourage companies to buy placements in supposedly independent rankings or publish artificial recommendations.

This approach creates serious credibility risks.

A ranking controlled by the company it promotes is not genuinely independent unless the relationship is clearly disclosed.

Similarly, fake customer reviews can mislead potential buyers and create legal problems.

European businesses should pay particular attention to transparency, consumer protection rules and accurate commercial communication.

A better strategy is to encourage authentic customer feedback, publish verifiable case studies and seek genuine coverage in relevant industry publications.

Trust should be earned through real business activity rather than manufactured signals.

## 6. Multilingual GEO: Why English Alone Is Not Enough

For companies targeting multiple European countries, multilingual optimization deserves special attention.

A website written entirely in English may serve international customers well, but it will not necessarily cover the needs of people searching in Polish, German, French or other local languages.

Search terminology can vary significantly between markets.

Even within the same language, customers in different countries may use different expressions.

For example, businesses targeting Germany and Austria may need to account for differences in terminology, search demand and regional expectations.

The same applies to English-speaking markets, including the United Kingdom and Ireland.

An effective international strategy should therefore include local keyword research, market-specific content, clear language targeting and properly implemented `hreflang` annotations where appropriate.

Avoid creating dozens of automatically translated pages that provide no additional value to local users.

Instead, develop content that reflects how customers in each country search for products and services.

## 7. Does llms.txt Help with AI Search Visibility?

The `llms.txt` file has attracted considerable attention in technical SEO discussions.

It is a proposed format designed to present important website information in a structured, machine-readable text file.

A business might use it to organize links to services, documentation, company details and other resources.

However, `llms.txt` is not a mandatory standard for Google Search, ChatGPT or Perplexity.

There is currently no reliable basis for claiming that simply adding this file guarantees improved visibility in AI-generated answers.

Google has also stated that no special AI-specific text file is required to appear in its AI search features.

For some websites, particularly those with extensive technical documentation, `llms.txt` may be worth exploring as an additional experiment.

It should not replace proper indexing, accessible content or a well-structured website.

## 8. How to Measure Your Business Visibility in AI Search

Start by identifying questions your potential customers are likely to ask.

For example:

“Which companies provide professional cosmetic laser repair in Poland?”

“Where can international students learn Polish in Wrocław?”

“How do I choose an SEO agency for a European business?”

Run these questions through different AI-powered search systems.

Record which businesses appear, which sources are cited and whether your brand is mentioned.

Then repeat the tests using a consistent set of prompts.

A single AI-generated answer is not a reliable ranking measurement.

Results may vary depending on the platform, wording, location, conversation context and time of the search.

Google Search Console can also provide useful search performance data, including information associated with Google's available AI search features.

However, Google search impressions must not be presented as the number of recommendations made by ChatGPT.

These are separate environments and require different measurement approaches.

For a more complete picture, businesses should also monitor relevant website visits, branded searches, enquiries and conversions where attribution is available.

## 9. Does GEO Replace Traditional SEO?

No.

GEO is not a shortcut around technical optimization, keyword research, content quality or brand credibility.

If search engines cannot properly access your website, adding AI-related terminology to your marketing strategy will not solve the problem.

Likewise, publishing dozens of generic AI-generated articles is unlikely to create meaningful value for customers.

A sustainable approach combines technical SEO, local search optimization, useful content, genuine business expertise and consistent information across relevant external sources.

GEO should be treated as an extension of this work.

## 10. A Practical GEO Starting Plan for European Businesses

You do not need an expensive AI visibility platform to begin.

Start with a few practical steps:

1. Audit the technical health and indexability of your website. 2. Create clear, dedicated pages for your core services. 3. Answer real customer questions with useful content. 4. Publish original case studies and evidence of your expertise. 5. Maintain accurate business profiles and encourage genuine reviews. 6. Identify the sources AI search systems cite in your industry. 7. Monitor your brand's visibility using a consistent methodology.

These actions cannot guarantee inclusion in AI-generated recommendations.

They can, however, improve the quality, clarity and availability of information about your business.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a business optimize its visibility in ChatGPT?**

Businesses can improve the availability and credibility of information about their brand in sources accessible to AI-powered search systems. However, there is no guaranteed method for obtaining a specific position in ChatGPT recommendations.

**Does GEO work for small businesses?**

Small businesses can benefit from improving their local information, publishing original content and building a credible online presence. Results depend on the market, competition and the specific AI system.

**Do I need a separate website for GEO?**

No. In most cases, GEO-related improvements can be implemented on an existing website.

**Does Schema.org guarantee AI citations?**

No. Structured data helps search engines interpret information, but it does not guarantee inclusion in AI-generated answers.

**How long does GEO take to produce results?**

There is no universal timeframe. Visibility depends on competition, website quality, available sources and how individual AI platforms retrieve and present information.
