SEO in 2026: How to Attract Customers Through Google and AI Search

A few years ago, most conversations about SEO started with search rankings. Businesses wanted to know where their websites appeared in Google, how many keywords ranked in the top ten and how much organic traffic they received.

Oleksandr Padalko25/11/20268 min read

In 2026, these metrics are no longer sufficient to understand the full effectiveness of customer acquisition through search.

People continue to use Google, but they increasingly encounter answers directly within search results. They also use ChatGPT, Perplexity and other AI-powered platforms to compare products, find service providers and make purchasing decisions.

A business may perform well in traditional organic search while appearing infrequently in generative answers. The opposite can also happen.

The journey between a customer's initial question and their purchasing decision is changing.

What is happening to traffic from Google?

In July 2025, Pew Research Center published a study of Google users' browsing behaviour. It examined the search activity of 900 US adults during March 2025.

Source: Pew Research Center, July 2025.

These figures should not be treated as universal measurements for every country, industry or search query. They nevertheless illustrate a meaningful change in user behaviour.

In February 2026, Ahrefs published updated research examining the relationship between AI Overviews and organic clicks. Using December 2025 data, the researchers found that AI Overview visibility correlated with a 58% lower average click-through rate for the top-ranking organic page.

This is an observed statistical association, not a guaranteed traffic loss for every website.

However, even a first-place organic ranking may now generate substantially fewer visits than businesses previously expected.

Zero-click search

Zero-click search existed long before generative artificial intelligence became widely available.

Google has provided weather forecasts, currency exchange rates, definitions and basic business information without requiring users to visit external websites for years.

Generative search extends this approach. Users can receive explanations of complex problems, product comparisons and recommendations about which services to consider.

Part of a customer's interaction with a brand may therefore happen outside the company's website.

Someone may discover a business through an AI answer, read customer reviews and then search for that business directly several days later.

Traditional web analytics may not capture the entire journey.

A decline in organic traffic does not necessarily indicate deteriorating business performance. Enquiries, sales and other meaningful conversions also need to be considered.

SEO, GEO and generative search

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) covers activities that improve website visibility in search engines.

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) describes approaches intended to improve the presence of information about businesses, products and services within generative responses.

These disciplines should complement one another rather than compete.

In May 2026, Google published official guidance on optimising websites for generative search features. The guidance reaffirmed the relevance of foundational SEO practices.

Technical accessibility, useful content, clear website organisation and reliable information remain important.

GEO cannot resolve indexing problems, incorrect canonical URLs or inadequate descriptions of a company's services.

There is also no special metadata tag or file that guarantees citation by an AI system.

The technical foundation of a website

Before investing in new articles or generative search optimisation, businesses should examine the technical condition of their websites.

Important pages must be accessible to search crawlers and eligible for indexing.

This involves reviewing HTTP status codes, robots.txt, robots directives, XML sitemaps, canonical URLs and internal links.

Multilingual websites also require properly organised language versions. Each version should have its own URL, appropriate language declaration and correct hreflang configuration.

Mobile usability is equally important. A potential customer opening the website on a smartphone should be able to find information, read service descriptions and contact the company without unnecessary obstacles.

Structured data helps describe page content and entities. Its presence does not guarantee rich search results or citations in generative answers.

Search intent matters more than keyword volume

Someone searching for “what is a CRM” is at a different stage of decision-making from someone looking for a CRM system for a ten-person sales team.

The first user is gathering information. The second is more likely to be considering a purchase.

The same distinction applies to searches such as “how does a heat pump work” and “heat pump installation cost in Wrocław”.

The first query calls for educational content. The second is better addressed by a service page explaining pricing, installation conditions and contact details.

Before creating a page, businesses should identify the problem the customer is attempting to solve.

Examining actual search results can also help determine whether a guide, comparison, service page or video would be the most appropriate format.

Keyword research remains useful, but it should support an understanding of customer needs rather than replace it.

Topic clusters instead of disconnected articles

A single page rarely answers every question a prospective customer might have.

For example, a solar installation company could create a central service page supported by articles about installation costs, investment payback periods, inverter selection, battery storage and maintenance.

Each page addresses a separate question. Together, they create a coherent information resource.

This approach is commonly described as topic clustering.

Internal links connect relevant pages, make navigation easier for visitors and help search engines discover related content.

There is no universal number of articles required to create an effective cluster. Some topics need only a few comprehensive pages, while others require considerably more coverage.

Publishing nearly identical pages solely to target variations of the same keyword provides little additional value.

Practical experience and E-E-A-T

Artificial intelligence makes it easier to prepare drafts, organise information and edit written materials.

However, generating a text quickly is not the same as acquiring years of professional experience.

A company writing about equipment installation can demonstrate actual completed projects, explain practical challenges and describe common mistakes.

An author comparing software products should distinguish between conclusions based on direct testing and information collected from public product descriptions.

Google uses the E-E-A-T concept to describe aspects of content quality:

  • Experience: first-hand practical knowledge.
  • Expertise: relevant professional competence.
  • Authoritativeness: recognised authority.
  • Trustworthiness: reliability and credibility.

E-E-A-T is not a single ranking factor with a fixed numerical weight.

Transparent authorship, verifiable sources, photographs, documentation and clear methodologies help readers assess the reliability of information.

Using AI does not automatically make content poor. Accuracy, usefulness and originality are what matter.

Preparing content for generative search

A page should be understandable to people first.

If its heading promises an answer to a specific question, the answer should not be buried beneath several paragraphs of introductory material.

Product specifications, service conditions, prices and limitations should be stated clearly.

Tables can make comparisons easier, while descriptive headings help organise complex subjects.

Generative systems may use Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), an approach that supplements generated responses with information retrieved from external sources.

Making information accessible does not guarantee that a platform will select it. Source selection depends on the system, its retrieval methods and the user's query.

There is also no universal rule stating that an article becomes outdated after three months.

Content should be updated when relevant facts, specifications, conditions or recommendations change.

Changing a publication date without improving the actual information serves little purpose.

Building a presence beyond the company website

A company's website is important, but it is not the only available source of information about the business.

Potential customers also use Google Maps, industry directories, professional communities, social media and review platforms.

Independent references can help customers verify business information.

Relevant external links remain useful within a broader SEO strategy.

Local businesses should maintain accurate Google Business Profile information, contact details, opening hours, addresses and service descriptions.

Buying large quantities of links or artificial mentions cannot replace a genuine professional reputation.

Measuring SEO effectiveness in 2026

Search rankings and organic traffic remain useful indicators, but they should be considered alongside business outcomes.

Businesses should monitor:

  • Search impressions, clicks and CTR.
  • Qualified customer enquiries.
  • Sales conversions.
  • Visits to commercial landing pages.
  • Branded search demand.
  • Measurable referrals from AI platforms.
  • Brand visibility in relevant generative answers.

Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4 can help measure some of these indicators.

However, not every AI-generated recommendation can be reliably attributed to a particular sale.

GEO reporting must acknowledge the limitations of available analytics rather than rely on unverifiable performance claims.

A practical plan for businesses

A modern search strategy can be organised into six stages.

  1. Conduct a technical SEO audit and resolve website accessibility issues.
  2. Identify the search intentions of prospective customers.
  3. Improve the quality and completeness of commercial landing pages.
  4. Develop topic clusters around genuine customer questions.
  5. Strengthen information reliability, professional reputation and external visibility.
  6. Regularly evaluate traffic, customer enquiries and sales.

This is not a one-time optimisation exercise. It is an ongoing process of developing a company's digital information presence.

Conclusion

SEO remains relevant in 2026. The environment in which it operates has changed.

Search engines and artificial intelligence increasingly contribute to purchasing decisions before customers visit a company's website.

Businesses need to consider not only their search rankings but also how accurately and comprehensively they are represented across digital channels.

A technically sound website, useful content, clear information architecture and demonstrated expertise remain essential to sustainable search visibility.

Ultimately, the effectiveness of SEO should still be measured by the customers it helps a business acquire.
