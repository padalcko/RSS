# 5 Lazy SEO Techniques That Can Actually Bring More Traffic

> Before spending more on SEO, check what your website already has. Five practical ways to improve search visibility without unnecessary work.

SEO

Some people seem to enjoy creating extra work for themselves. I've met quite a few of them in SEO.

Oleksandr Padalko · 20/11/2026 · 7 min read

You can spend months researching keywords, ordering articles, analysing competitors, building links and producing impressive monthly reports.

Or you can open Google Search Console, look at your existing website and discover that some of your best opportunities have been sitting there all along.

Sometimes the second approach is more useful.

I'm not suggesting that SEO is easy. And I'm certainly not promising first place on Google after five quick fixes.

But before spending money on another large SEO campaign, there are a few things worth checking.

I call them lazy SEO techniques.

Not because you can do nothing, but because doing the right work often matters more than doing a lot of work.

## 1. Stop hunting for new keywords before checking the ones you already rank for

Imagine you run an appliance repair business in London.

You want more customers from Google.

The usual approach might involve building a huge keyword list, creating new landing pages and commissioning twenty blog posts.

But here's a question.

Have you checked which searches already show your website?

Perhaps your page is currently ranking eighth for "washing machine repair London".

Google already knows the page exists. It considers the page relevant enough to display it.

It just isn't ranking particularly high yet.

So why not look at that page before creating ten more?

Does it explain the service clearly? Are the prices or pricing process easy to understand? Can customers see which areas you cover? Is your phone number easy to find?

Perhaps the page needs better content.

Perhaps its title is too vague.

Perhaps competitors answer customers' questions more effectively.

**What should you do?**

Open the Performance report in Google Search Console.

Look at queries generating impressions and identify pages that may have room to improve.

Queries with average positions around 4 to 20 can be a useful starting point.

There is nothing magical about that range. It simply helps narrow down the opportunities.

Remember to consider impressions, clicks, search intent and commercial value, not just average position.

## 2. Audit your website. Seriously, just check it

It sounds obvious.

Yet I've seen websites receiving ongoing SEO work while basic technical problems remain untouched.

Incorrect canonical tags. Multiple pages with identical titles. Internal links pointing to 404 pages. Important content accidentally blocked from indexing.

Meanwhile, the monthly SEO report looks fantastic.

You can spend hours discussing Google's algorithms, but if an important page cannot be indexed, expecting it to perform in organic search is rather optimistic.

Of course, not every technical warning is a ranking problem.

A missing meta description, for example, does not automatically mean Google will penalise your website.

The important thing is to identify issues that genuinely affect accessibility, indexing and usability.

**What should you do?**

Check HTTP status codes, indexing, page titles, headings, canonical tags, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, internal links and structured data.

Then prioritise.

Don't waste time fixing every minor warning simply because an audit tool has highlighted it in red.

Start with the problems that actually prevent search engines and users from accessing useful content.

## 3. Please stop calling every page "Our Services"

This is one of my favourite examples.

You visit a company website.

Beautiful design. Great photography. Smooth animations. Everything looks professional.

Then you open a service page.

The headline says: "Innovative Solutions for Your Business".

What solutions? For which businesses? What do you actually sell?

Nobody knows.

Sometimes it feels as though companies are afraid to explain what they do.

But visitors shouldn't have to solve a marketing puzzle.

If your company repairs commercial air conditioning systems, a heading such as "Commercial Air Conditioning Repair" is usually much clearer than "Innovative Climate Solutions for Modern Businesses".

The second version may look impressive in a presentation.

But someone with a broken air conditioner is looking for a repair service.

**What should you do?**

Review the title tags and H1 headings on your important pages.

Make sure they clearly describe what visitors will find.

Don't cram every possible keyword into a single headline.

A good heading should make sense to an ordinary customer, not just an SEO specialist looking at a spreadsheet.

## 4. Your page might be answering the wrong question

Imagine you own a bicycle shop.

You create a page about mountain bikes.

Half the content explains your delivery service. Another section tells the history of your company. The rest talks about how passionate your team is.

The actual bikes barely get a mention.

Then you wonder why the page isn't performing well for relevant searches.

The problem may have nothing to do with keyword density.

Perhaps the page simply doesn't give people what they came for.

Someone searching for "buy mountain bike" probably wants to see available models, specifications, prices, stock information and purchasing options.

They don't need five paragraphs about your company's mission.

Search intent matters.

Is the visitor trying to buy something? Compare options? Find a specialist? Check a price? Learn how to solve a problem?

The answer should influence what you put on the page.

**What should you do?**

Search Google for the query you're targeting.

Look at the types of pages and information being shown.

Then examine your own page.

Can visitors actually complete the task they came to do?

If not, improve the content.

And please don't add another three thousand words just because a competitor has a longer article.

Sometimes a useful comparison table, clear pricing and an easy way to get in touch are far more valuable.

## 5. A page with no organic traffic isn't necessarily useless

This is where some SEO advice becomes dangerous.

You may have heard that pages receiving no organic traffic should simply be deleted.

The logic sounds reasonable.

If nobody visits a page from Google, why keep it?

But consider your warranty information page.

It might receive almost no organic search traffic, yet customers still need it.

Or imagine a highly specialised service that only a handful of people search for each month.

One enquiry could be worth thousands to your business.

Would you really delete that page?

No organic traffic doesn't automatically mean no business value.

And the opposite is also true.

A blog post attracting thousands of irrelevant visitors may contribute very little to your business.

**What should you do?**

Identify pages with low organic traffic and review them individually.

Some may need updating.

Others could be combined with related content.

Sometimes a redirect makes sense.

And sometimes the best decision is to leave the page alone.

Don't turn SEO optimisation into demolition work.

## So why are these techniques lazy?

Because they focus on what you already have.

Your website exists. Your pages exist. Google may already be showing some of them to potential customers.

Before launching an expensive campaign, it makes sense to check whether those existing pages are doing their jobs properly.

Sometimes a clearer heading helps.

Sometimes you need to fix a technical problem.

Sometimes a few useful paragraphs make the difference between a confusing page and one that answers a customer's question.

And sometimes an audit reveals that the website needs much more substantial work.

That's useful information too.

SEO isn't magic. There is no secret button that makes Google fall in love with your website.

It's a process of improving technical quality, content and the experience people have when they visit.

## Where should you start?

I'd start with a [technical audit](https://raccoon-studio.com.ua/en/#services) and Google Search Console.

Then I'd choose five pages with genuine potential.

Not fifty. Not two hundred.

Just five.

Check their content, headings, indexing and how well they match what visitors are looking for.

Make sensible improvements and monitor the results.
